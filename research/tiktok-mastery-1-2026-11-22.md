## 1. How the For You feed works

### 1.1 The pipeline, as TikTok describes it

TikTok's Safety Center page describes the recommendation system as a
series of machine-learning models going through five steps
`[OFFICIAL — TikTok Safety Center, "Introduction to the TikTok
recommendation system", read 2026-11-22]`:

1. **Selecting videos for recommendation.** The system retrieves a
   large number of videos that have passed TikTok's initial content
   moderation processes and are eligible for recommendation to the For
   You feed. Only videos that pass moderation enter the pool.
2. **Predicting.** For each video, the system estimates the likelihood
   the viewer will interact with it — like, share, comment, skip,
   follow the author, finish the video, favourite it, spend a certain
   amount of time on it, or tap its soundtrack. The individual
   predictions combine into an overall score.
3. **Ranking.** Videos are sorted by prediction score, highest first.
   The For You feed is built from the top-ranked videos.
4. **Similarity check.** If the top-ranked videos are too similar to
   each other — for example, they use the same sound — the system
   replaces some of them to keep the feed varied.
5. **Recommendation rules.** Extra rules are applied at different
   points: for example, the feed should include content from creators
   in the viewer's region, and content created by accounts aged under
   16 is ineligible for recommendation.

### 1.2 The signals, as TikTok describes them

TikTok's Newsroom post (June 2020) groups the signals into three sets
`[OFFICIAL — TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou",
19 June 2020, read 2026-11-22]`:

- **User interactions:** videos the viewer likes or shares, accounts
  they follow, comments they post, and content they create.
- **Video information:** details like captions, sounds, and hashtags.
- **Device and account settings:** language preference, country
  setting, device type. These receive **lower weight** than the other
  signals, "since users don't actively express these as preferences".

**Weights — the one comparison TikTok states explicitly:** a strong
indicator of interest, "such as whether a user finishes watching a
longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than
a weak indicator, such as whether the video's viewer and creator are
both in the same country" `[OFFICIAL — same Newsroom post]`. Finishing
a longer video is the strongest signal TikTok names. That is the
official basis for the factory's deciding metric (completion rate).

The Safety Center appendix adds further signals TikTok says it uses
`[OFFICIAL — TikTok Safety Center, recommendation-system page]`:
the viewer's region, device language, device operating system, videos
recently watched / finished / liked / skipped, follow relationships;
and about the video: posting time, posting region, the author's
language setting, the soundtrack, the length, and the hashtags.

### 1.3 What TikTok says is NOT a direct factor

"While a video is likely to receive more views if posted by an account
that has more followers, by virtue of that account having built up a
larger follower base, **neither follower count nor whether the account
has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors in the
recommendation system**" `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom, 19 June 2020]`. Each
video is judged by the reactions of the people who actually see it.
For a new account with zero followers, this is the single most
important confirmed fact in this module: the factory does not need an
audience to reach an audience; it needs videos that hold the viewers
they are shown to.

### 1.4 Diversity and safety rules that shape the feed

- The feed generally will **not** show two videos in a row made with
  the same sound or by the same creator; TikTok does not recommend
  duplicated content, content the viewer has already seen, or spam
  `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom, 19 June 2020]`.
- The system deliberately intersperses diverse content, including
  videos that do not match the viewer's expressed interests, so the
  viewer can discover new categories `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom]`.
- Content that violates the Community Guidelines is removed; content
  not suitable for a broad audience is made **ineligible for
  recommendation** — it stays up, but never enters the For You pool
  `[OFFICIAL — TikTok Safety Center]`. Videos just uploaded or under
  review, and spam seeking to artificially increase traffic, may also
  be ineligible `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom]`.

### 1.5 What this means for the factory (confirmed)

- **Completion is the deciding signal.** TikTok says so itself. The
  experiment protocol's choice of completion as a joint deciding
  metric is officially grounded, not a guess.
- **Every video starts equal.** Follower count is not a direct factor.
  A new account's first video is judged on its viewers' reactions.
- **The moderation gate comes first.** A video that fails moderation
  or is FYF-ineligible never reaches the ranking stage. Policy
  compliance is a distribution requirement, not a nicety.
- **Similarity is a constraint.** Videos that are too alike (same
  sound, same structure, back-to-back) are filtered. A factory that
  publishes near-identical videos daily risks having them de-duplicated
  in the feed — a reason the format needs visible variety between
  rounds, not just new questions.

## 2. Discovery surfaces: For You, Following, Search, topics

TikTok has more than one surface, and they behave differently:

- **For You** is the main surface: interest-based, per-user, driven by
  the pipeline in §1. It is where most users spend their time
  `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom, 19 June 2020]`.
- **Following** is the social surface: videos from accounts the viewer
  follows. It rewards having followers; For You does not require them.
- **Search** is the intent surface: the viewer types a query. It is
  where TikTok SEO operates (§3). TikTok's Newsroom states that
  "search powers discovery on TikTok, helping people explore, learn,
  and dive into topics they're curious about" `[OFFICIAL — TikTok
  Newsroom, "Get inspired with Creator Search Insights", read
  2026-11-22]`.
- **Topics / Discover** is the browsing surface: categories, trending
  topics, sounds, effects, hashtags. Exploring them is one of the ways
  TikTok says viewers refine their own recommendations `[OFFICIAL —
  Newsroom, 19 June 2020]`.

The factory's videos must work on For You first (that is where a new
account is found) and on Search second (that is where a video keeps
earning views after the For You push fades). The two surfaces want
different things: For You wants a video that holds whoever is shown
it; Search wants a video that matches what someone typed.

## 3. TikTok SEO — what is confirmed, and what is practitioner inference

### 3.1 Confirmed by TikTok itself

- **Video information feeds the system.** TikTok's official signal
  list includes "captions, sounds, and hashtags" as video information
  `[OFFICIAL — Newsroom, 19 June 2020; Safety Center appendix]`.
- **Search value is a paid metric.** The Creator Rewards formula
  includes **search value**: "a metric assigned to content based on
  popular search terms. Content that aligns with in-demand search
  topics increases its value for searchers" `[OFFICIAL — TikTok
  Newsroom, "Introducing the New Creator Rewards Program", read
  2026-11-22]`. Search alignment is not just discoverability — it is
  part of how TikTok pays.
- **Creator Search Insights exists and is TikTok's own tool for this.**
  It shows "topics people are searching for on TikTok", sortable by
  category or by "For You" (topics related to the content you create),
  with a **content-gap filter**: "topics [that] are searched for often,
  but aren't featured in a large number of videos on TikTok"
  `[OFFICIAL — TikTok Newsroom, "Get inspired with Creator Search
  Insights", read 2026-11-22]`. Topics carry a popularity score or a
  "Recommended Topic" label. The tool is reached by typing "Creator
  Search Insights" in the search bar, and is available in select
  regions.
- **Search value connects to Creator Rewards.** "Search value will also
  factor into the Creator Rewards Program, which means creating content
  related to in-demand search topics will increase creators' content's
  search value" `[OFFICIAL — same Newsroom post]`.

### 3.2 Practitioner inference — plausible, flagged, not official

The widely repeated "three layers" model — the target term should
appear in (1) the caption, (2) the on-screen text, and (3) the spoken
audio — comes from practitioner guides, not from TikTok's own pages
`[PRACTITIONER — SocialPilot, "TikTok SEO", read 2026-11-22; tiklab,
"TikTok SEO: how captions and keywords get you found", read
2026-11-22; Hootsuite, "How the TikTok algorithm works in 2026", read
2026-11-22]`. Hootsuite (practitioner, updated 5 days before this
research) states the algorithm "analyzes captions, voiceovers, and
on-screen text to categorize your content and surface it in search",
and that "search signals carry more weight" after TikTok added Search
Highlights. The same guides report that TikTok transcribes audio and
reads on-screen text (OCR) for indexing. **TikTok's official pages
read for this module do not state that audio transcription or OCR
indexing happens.** The inference is consistent with TikTok's confirmed
facts (captions/sounds/hashtags as video information; search value as a
metric), and is corroborated across independent practitioner sources —
but it is not an official fact. Treat it as a hypothesis to test in
our own experiments, not as a rule.

Other practitioner claims, same status `[PRACTITIONER]`:
- Front-load the primary keyword in the first sentence of the caption;
  words near the start rank slightly higher.
- 3–5 specific hashtags per video, not 30; hashtags are categorization
  labels.
- Put the target term on screen in the first 3 seconds, and speak it
  in the first 10 seconds of audio.
- Keyword research: TikTok search autocomplete and Creative Center
  "Keyword Insights" show real user queries; target 1–2 long-tail
  keywords per video.
- "49% of consumers use TikTok as a search engine" (Hootsuite, citing
  its own research) — a practitioner statistic, not TikTok's.

### 3.3 What this means for the factory (confirmed + testable)

- **Confirmed:** every video needs a topic phrase that real people
  search for, and that phrase belongs in the caption. Search value is
  paid, so this is a revenue decision, not a metadata chore.
- **Confirmed:** Creator Search Insights (content gaps) is the
  official tool for choosing topics. Using it is a human step — it
  lives in the app, in select regions — and it belongs in the idea
  queue routine (Module 4).
- **Testable hypothesis (not a rule):** the same topic phrase in the
  caption, on screen in the first 3 seconds, and spoken in the first
  10 seconds improves search discovery. Test it as a variable in a
  later experiment; do not write it into `DESIGN_RULES.md` yet.

## 4. Creator Rewards — eligibility, mechanics, and what is paid

All of this section is `[OFFICIAL]` unless flagged. Sources: TikTok
Support, "Creator Rewards Program" (current page, read 2026-11-22);
TikTok Newsroom, "Introducing the New Creator Rewards Program" (read
2026-11-22).

### 4.1 Who is eligible

- Based in a country where the program is available.
- Account in good standing, with no history of repeatedly or
  irresponsibly violating the Community Guidelines or Terms of Service,
  and no malicious or fraudulent activity.
- **Personal Account.** Business accounts and accounts belonging to
  political or government organizations are not eligible.
- Authentic account information (real name and date of birth).
- At least **18 years old** (19 in South Korea).
- At least **10,000 followers**.
- At least **100,000 video views in the last 30 days**.
- Posts videos that are at least one minute long.

After acceptance, the creator must not engage in fraud (fake views,
inflated followers), must post original and authentic content, must not
tamper with the program, the reward system, or the recommendation
system, and must not create malicious software to artificially register,
post, or increase likes, follows, views, comments, or shares. Violation
means permanent or temporary removal from the program.

**Discrepancy recorded honestly:** TikTok's legal "Creator Rewards
Program Terms" page (the JP/KR regional version found in search)
states **50,000** authentic followers. The global Help Center page and
the Newsroom announcement both state 10,000. The 50,000 figure appears
to be a regional terms variant (Japan/Korea). The factory follows the
global Help Center figure (10,000) for a US account — and verifies the
real number in TikTok Studio → Monetization when the account exists.
Do not treat either number as settled until Studio shows it.

**Eligible countries — not settled from official sources.** TikTok's
Help Center page does not enumerate the countries in the text read.
Secondary sources disagree: one list names 8 countries (US, UK,
Germany, France, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Brazil); another names 12
(adding Canada, Italy, Spain, Australia). Recorded as unknown until
Studio shows it for the actual account.

### 4.2 What a video must be, to earn

- Original and high-quality content, "filmed, designed, and produced
  entirely by yourself".
- At least one minute long.
- Uploaded **after** joining the program.
- At least **1,000 qualified For You feed views**.

**Qualified views** are unique video views from the For You feed,
excluding fraud, paid views, disliked views, views with less than 5
seconds watched, promoted views, and artificial views. Views from the
same account count only once per video. Views from people who said
"not interested" are not counted.

**What is NOT original** (Help Center, verbatim categories): Duet or
Stitch videos; content copied completely from others, including with
others' watermarks; content reproduced from others with only slight
modifications (sped up, filters, fixed texts, stickers); content
containing other people's videos or pictures without new and personal
ideas; **looping videos, single or multiple photos, or only text
overlays**; lip syncs or copyrighted music playing for over one minute
(at risk of muting). The factory's design rule (60+ s, narration +
motion + captions, never text-only) is exactly the boundary TikTok
draws here.

### 4.3 How rewards are calculated

Rewards are calculated from **qualified views and rewards per 1,000
qualified views (RPM)**. Eligible videos start collecting rewards at
1,000 qualified For You views. **Standard Reward** is based on
qualified views and RPM; **Additional Reward** is based on "how
well-crafted, engaging, and specialized content is".

The Newsroom announcement states the optimized formula focuses on four
core metrics `[OFFICIAL]`:
- **Originality** — quality content unique to the creator, showcasing
  their point of view or creative thought process.
- **Play duration** — watch time and finish rate. "The new formula
  rewards accounts with content that is clear, and engaging, rather
  than favoring accounts with an excessive amount of videos."
- **Audience engagement** — likes, comments, and shares.
- **Search value** — content aligned with in-demand search topics
  (§3.1).

The formula also "automatically reward[s] creators for their account's
ad value determined by their community's ad watchtime".

**TikTok publishes no RPM.** The Help Center page does not state one,
and the Newsroom announcement does not state one. Creator-reported
ranges ($0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views) are `[UNPROVEN]` for
planning: no revenue forecast is ever written as fact. The dashboard
shows per-video RPM and qualified views — but only once the account is
in the program.

**Other confirmed facts:** the community "now spends 50% of their time
on TikTok watching videos longer than one minute" (Newsroom) — the
official basis for the factory's 60-second minimum being a growth
choice as well as a monetization rule. Application reply comes within
approximately 3 days. Accounts and videos are periodically reviewed;
rewards already collected on a removed video are deducted; appeals must
be submitted within 30 days.

### 4.4 What this means for the factory (confirmed)

- The factory's videos already meet the *content* side of eligibility
  (original, ≥60 s, narration + motion + captions). The *audience*
  side (10,000 followers, 100,000 views/30 days) is entirely unmet and
  is the real work.
- **Play duration and search value are paid.** They are not vanity
  metrics. Every creative decision should be judged against the four
  formula metrics: originality, play duration, engagement, search
  value.
- **Volume is not rewarded.** The formula explicitly does not favour
  "an excessive amount of videos". One good video beats five thin
  ones. This confirms the experiment protocol's 1-video/day cadence
  and its rule against volume without learning.
- **The account must stay clean.** Five video violations in 30 days,
  or five total account violations, are reported (secondary) to make an
  account ineligible — the Help Center itself says only that repeated
  or irresponsible violations disqualify. Either way: a strike is a
  monetization event, not a minor inconvenience.

## 5. Policies — what the factory may and may not publish

Source: TikTok Community Guidelines overview, **released 25 August
2026, effective 24 September 2026** `[OFFICIAL — tiktok.com/safety,
read 2026-11-22]`. This is a recent version; the previous version was
released 14 August 2025. The factory's policy knowledge must be
re-checked whenever TikTok releases a new version.

### 5.1 The rules that bind the factory directly

- **Violent and criminal behaviour; hate speech; violent and hateful
  organisations; youth and adult sexual abuse; human trafficking;
  harassment and bullying; suicide and self-harm; disordered eating;
  dangerous activity and challenges; body exposure and sexualised
  behaviours; shocking and graphic content; animal abuse** — all not
  allowed. The factory's standing content boundaries (STATE.md) cover
  these; the Guidelines are the authoritative text behind them.
- **Misinformation:** not allowed where it "could cause significant
  harm to individuals or society". A wrong trivia answer presented as
  fact is a quality failure; a wrong *claim* presented as fact in a
  sensitive area is a policy failure. Fact-checking (D9) is therefore
  a compliance function, not just a quality function.
- **Edited media and AI-generated content (AIGC):** "We require clear
  labelling when AI or editing is used to realistically depict people
  or scenes. We don't allow AIGC that misleads about matters of public
  importance or that harms individuals." (See §5.2.)
- **Unoriginal content and intellectual property:** reposts of
  copyrighted or trademarked material without permission are not
  allowed. The factory draws every icon in code and writes every
  question and riddle itself for exactly this reason.
- **Deceptive behaviour and fake engagement:** accounts that mislead
  or try to manipulate the platform, and the trade of services that
  artificially boost engagement or trick the recommendation system,
  are not allowed. Buying views or followers is excluded twice over:
  by the Guidelines and by the Creator Rewards rules.
- **Frauds and scams; commercial disclosure:** promoting a product,
  brand, or business requires TikTok's content disclosure setting.
- **Personal information; platform security:** no sharing personal
  information that could lead to harm; no attempts to hack or
  reverse-engineer TikTok's systems.

### 5.2 AI-generated content — the labelling rule

Source: TikTok Support, "About AI-generated content" `[OFFICIAL —
support.tiktok.com, read 2026-11-22]`; Community Guidelines §
"Edited Media and AI-Generated Content (AIGC)" `[OFFICIAL]`.

- **Definition:** AI-generated content is images, video, and/or audio
  "generated or modified by artificial intelligence", including
  artificial visuals, videos, or sounds that may portray realistic
  human likenesses or depictions in a particular artistic style.
- **Two tiers.** TikTok *encourages* labelling content that is
  completely generated or significantly edited by AI. TikTok
  *requires* labelling all AI-generated content that contains
  realistic images, audio, and video.
- **What counts as significantly edited:** real images/video used as
  source material but modified by AI beyond minor corrections,
  including synthetic images/video where the primary subjects are
  portrayed doing something they didn't do, saying something they
  didn't say (for example, AI-generated speech), or their appearance
  substantially altered so the original subject is no longer
  recognizable (for example, an AI face-swap).
- **How to label:** the creator label ("creator labeled as
  AI-generated"), applied at posting; or disclosure in the post itself
  (text, hashtag sticker, context in the description). TikTok may also
  apply an **auto label** ("AI-generated") when it identifies content
  as completely generated or significantly edited — including via
  Content Credentials (C2PA) metadata. Once auto-labelled, the label
  cannot be removed.
- **Misleading labelling is a violation:** labelling unaltered content
  as AI-generated to mislead violates the Terms of Service and may
  result in removal.
- **What this means for the factory:** the factory's videos are
  AI-narrated (Kokoro TTS voice) and code-rendered. The spoken
  narration is AI-generated speech. Under TikTok's own definition,
  AI-generated speech is an example of significantly edited content.
  The experiment protocol already requires the AI-generated label on
  every video (in-app toggle, or the `is_aigc` flag via the API).
  This module confirms that requirement is officially grounded — and
  adds the nuance that TikTok *requires* the label for realistic
  AI audio/video, not merely encourages it. No video goes out
  without the label. **Open point:** whether a synthetic voice
  reading an original script, with no real person depicted, triggers
  the *required* tier or only the *encouraged* tier is not settled by
  the pages read — the factory labels anyway, which satisfies both
  readings. Flagged as a question to re-check in the Guidelines'
  detailed AIGC section, not as a gap in the rule.

### 5.3 Enforcement — removal, age-restriction, FYF-ineligibility

TikTok enforces in three ways `[OFFICIAL — Community Guidelines
overview]`:
- **Remove** content that falls under the "Not Allowed" rules.
- **Age-restrict** content not appropriate for under-18s (viewable
  only by adults).
- **Make FYF-ineligible** content that falls under the "FYF
  Ineligible" standards — it stays up, but "won't be recommended in
  the FYF". The overview page links to the full FYF-standards list;
  the detailed list was **not** read in full for this module and is
  recorded as partially verified (see §6, unknowns). What is confirmed
  from the Safety Center page: content not suitable for a broad
  audience, content under review, and spam are ineligible; and from
  the Guidelines overview, the three enforcement tiers exist.

## 6. What TikTok does NOT disclose — labelled unknowns

These are not gaps in this research. They are gaps in what TikTok
publishes. Do not fill them with guesses.

1. **Ranking weights and the formula.** TikTok names the signals and
   one weight comparison (completion of a longer video > same-country).
   It does not publish weights, thresholds, or the scoring formula.
   Any claim to know them is `[UNPROVEN]`.
2. **Initial distribution.** TikTok does not state how many viewers a
   new video is first shown to, or what threshold it must pass to be
   shown more widely. Claims of a "200-view test" or similar are
   `[UNPROVEN]`.
3. **Search ranking weights.** TikTok confirms search value exists and
   Creator Search Insights shows demand. It does not publish how
   search results are ranked, or how much weight captions vs on-screen
   text vs spoken audio carry. The three-layer practice is
   `[PRACTITIONER]`, not official.
4. **RPM.** TikTok publishes no RPM. Any revenue forecast built on a
   creator-reported RPM is `[UNPROVEN]`.
5. **Review and audit timelines.** TikTok publishes no firm time for
   app review or the Content Posting API audit (see the connection
   research). The Creator Rewards application reply is "approximately
   3 days" `[OFFICIAL]`.
6. **The full FYF-ineligible list.** The Guidelines overview confirms
   the category exists and links to it; the detailed list was not read
   in full for this module. Partially verified — read before relying
   on it.
7. **Eligible-country list for Creator Rewards.** The Help Center page
   does not enumerate it in the text read; secondary lists disagree
   (8 vs 12 countries). Unknown until Studio shows it.
8. **Whether the 10,000-follower threshold is universal.** The global
   Help Center says 10,000; the JP/KR legal terms say 50,000. Regional
   variation exists; verify for the actual account.
9. **How "search value" is computed.** TikTok names it as a formula
   metric and says it is "assigned to content based on popular search
   terms". It does not publish the computation.
10. **How much the AI-generated label affects distribution.** TikTok
    does not state that labelling reduces reach. The niche discovery
    recorded that labelling "does not affect distribution if guidelines
    are followed" (secondary summary of Creator Academy). Unverified
    from TikTok's own pages read here — do not cite it as official.

## 7. What this changes in the factory

Concrete consequences of Module 1. Each item names whether it is a
rule adopted now or a hypothesis queued for testing.

### Rules adopted now (official evidence)

1. **Completion stays a deciding metric.** TikTok itself names
   finishing a longer video as the strongest signal. No change to
   `EXPERIMENTS.md` §4 — the protocol was already right, and now it
   is officially grounded.
2. **The AI-generated label stays mandatory on every video.**
   Confirmed and strengthened: TikTok *requires* it for realistic AI
   audio/video. Already in the publishing checklist
   (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §3); no change needed, but the reason is now
   cited here.
3. **The 60-second minimum stays.** Confirmed twice: Creator Rewards
   requires ≥1 minute, and TikTok reports 50% of viewing time is on
   videos longer than one minute. Already in `DESIGN_RULES.md` via
   the niche decision; now cited here.
4. **Originality is a distribution rule, not a preference.** Text-only
   content, loops, and copied material are excluded from Rewards and
   can be FYF-ineligible. The factory's "narration + motion +
   captions, never text-only" rule stands, now with the official
   text behind it.
5. **No volume strategy.** The Rewards formula explicitly does not
   favour "an excessive amount of videos". The 1-video/day cadence in
   `EXPERIMENTS.md` §8 stands.
6. **Policy version tracked.** The Community Guidelines version in
   force is **released 25 August 2026, effective 24 September 2026**.
   Re-check on any new release. Recorded here and in `LEARNING.md`
   as a re-check trigger.

### Hypotheses queued for testing (practitioner evidence — not rules)

7. **H-SEO-1 — one topic phrase, three places.** A video whose topic
   phrase appears in the caption's first line, on screen in the first
   3 seconds, and spoken in the first 10 seconds earns more search
   traffic than the same video without that alignment. Test as a
   variable after EXP-001/EXP-002 report. *Requires a change to the
   build: the caption, the opening on-screen text, and the opening
   spoken line must be written from the same topic phrase.*
8. **H-SEO-2 — content-gap topics.** Videos built on a Creator Search
   Insights content-gap topic earn more views per video than videos
   built on a guessed topic. Test once the account exists and the
   tool is accessible (human step; Module 4 owns the routine).
9. **H-HOOK-1 — the first 3 seconds decide the swipe.** A question
   stated on screen in the first 3 seconds (with the topic phrase)
   raises completion versus the current title-card opening. This is
   the first hook variable in the experiment queue (Module 2 owns the
   hook taxonomy).
10. **H-VAR-1 — visible variety between rounds.** Because the
    similarity check replaces videos that are too alike, two
    consecutive rounds that differ only in their questions may be
    de-duplicated in the feed. Test a visible variation (different
    opening beat, different icon emphasis) between consecutive
    videos of the same format.

### Changes to EXP-001 / EXP-002 (the first experiments)

- **Caption practice (adopted now):** each video's caption opens with
  its topic phrase in natural language ("Trivia quiz: 5 questions,
  no Googling" / "3 riddles, 10 seconds each"), then 2–3 lines of
  context, then 3–5 specific hashtags. This is practitioner practice
  applied to an officially confirmed signal (captions are video
  information) — the *practice* is a hypothesis, the *signal* is
  official. The caption is written by the factory and recorded in the
