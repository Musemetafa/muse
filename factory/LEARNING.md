# Factory Learning Program — becoming a professional

**Status:** created 2026-11-22; **restructured 2026-11-22** after Mohamed's
second correction (see "The open-ended principle" below). This is the
factory's FIRST responsibility.
**Honest starting point:** the factory currently has **NO proven professional
expertise** in TikTok, SEO, audience understanding, or competitive content
creation. That is Mohamed's own assessment (2026-11-22 ~15:30 UTC), and the
factory accepts it. Nothing in this file claims otherwise. The prototypes
prove the factory can *render* a 90-second narrated video; they prove
nothing about whether anyone will watch it, finish it, or come back for
another one.

---

## The open-ended principle — read this first

**The domains listed in this file are a starting point only. They are not
a map of the field, and they are not a boundary on what the factory
researches or learns.** Mohamed's list of domains, skills, and examples —
and anything any assistant suggests — is a floor, not a ceiling. We do
not know in advance everything that can be discovered. The factory's
responsibility is to discover the field in the widest possible picture,
including aspects, capabilities, techniques, and opportunities that did
not occur to anyone, and that we may not even know exist.

**No list, guidance, strategy, recipe, school, or method — including
this file — is a ceiling on the program's ambition.** A domain, a queue
position, or a rule in this file is the factory's current best
understanding, not a limit. The program must be able to rediscover
itself, expand, and develop. That is not a slogan: the discovery
protocol below is a permanent practice with mechanics, a schedule, and
a log, and the queue is explicitly open and revisable by evidence.

The goal is not an agent that knows how to execute a set of known
tasks. It is an agent that continuously discovers capabilities we did
not know we needed, acquires them, and turns them into real strength
in the factory and the commercial project. Expanding the boundaries of
its knowledge is the factory's responsibility — not staying inside the
boundaries anyone drew for it.

## Purpose

Turn "become a professional" from a wish into a documented, testable
system — and turn "discover what we don't know" from a wish into a
standing practice. Every domain below has a level, key questions, a
source hierarchy, and a defined way its knowledge becomes a factory
capability. Learning is permanent work, not a phase: there is no level
at which the factory stops learning, and no list at which it stops
looking.

**Scope note:** Mohamed listed the domains in his 2026-11-22 message.
Two further domains were added by the factory because the work
revealed them (D9, D10). Discovery Scan 1 (2026-11-22) added seven
more (D11–D17) — see the Discovered-domains log. The factory is
responsible for discovering the rest. If a future scan finds a domain
that makes an existing one look small, the queue changes. That is the
system working, not failing.

## Knowledge levels

| Level | Meaning | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| **NONE** | No proven expertise. The factory must not act as if it has any. | The default. Every domain starts here. |
| **LEARNING** | Studying the domain from sources; no factory decision rests on it yet. | Sources read and cited in a module report. |
| **TESTED** | The knowledge was turned into a hypothesis and run as an experiment. | Hypothesis + experiment in `EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`. |
| **PROVEN** | Our own account data confirmed it. | Measured result against the pre-registered rule. |
| **RULE** | Written into factory law (`DESIGN_RULES.md`, `EXPERIMENTS.md`, or a module). | The rule names the evidence that earned it. |

A domain reaches RULE only through our own measured results, or through an
official platform document that states the rule directly (a policy is a
rule the moment TikTok publishes it). A practitioner claim never becomes a
rule on its own authority — it becomes a hypothesis first.

**Current level in every domain below: NONE.** Some adjacent factory
knowledge exists (the render pipeline, the safe-zone system, the
experiment protocol) but none of it is professional expertise in the
domains listed here. Do not let the existence of working code be mistaken
for the existence of professional judgement.

## Source hierarchy — what counts, and how much

| Tier | Source | Weight | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Official platform documentation and policy** — TikTok Help Center, TikTok Newsroom, TikTok for Developers, TikTok Safety Center, Google Search Central. | Decisive. A policy is a rule the moment it is published. | `[OFFICIAL]` |
| 2 | **Published platform data and research** — TikTok's own published statistics, Creator Academy data, peer-reviewed or large-sample published studies. | Strong, but check the sample and the date. | `[DATA]` |
| 3 | **Credible practitioner experiments with data** — a practitioner who shows their numbers, their method, and their sample size. | A hypothesis, never a rule. Test it ourselves. | `[PRACTITIONER]` |
| 4 | **Claims, opinions, marketing** — tool blogs, "guru" advice, uncited assertions, AI-video-tool marketing. | Unproven. Flag it and do not build on it. | `[UNPROVEN]` |

**Tagging rules (binding):**
- Every substantive claim in every module report carries a tag and a date
  read. A claim without a tag is an opinion, not knowledge.
- `[OFFICIAL]` vs `[PRACTITIONER]` vs `[DATA]` vs `[UNPROVEN]` is a
  statement about the *source*, not about whether the claim is true.
  An official page can be outdated; a practitioner claim can be right.
  The tag says how much the factory may lean on it before testing.
- **Knowledge expires.** When TikTok changes a policy, a formula, or a
  feature, every claim that depended on it is re-checked. The re-check
  trigger is recorded in the module report. A claim that was true in
  2026 and is still cited in 2027 without a re-check is a stale claim,
  not knowledge.
- **Never present an unproven claim as a rule.** If the factory writes a
  rule, the rule names its evidence: an official source, or our own
  measured result.
- **Never copy a famous creator's style, never follow a ready-made
  recipe.** The factory's job is to discover its own creative
  opportunities and build a distinctive identity. Studying what others
  do is research; copying what they do is the opposite of the brief.
  A module may analyse a format's mechanics; it may not prescribe
  imitating a specific creator.

## The learning loop

```
Learn (module) → Turn into a capability or a testable hypothesis
  → Experiment (EXPERIMENTS.md) → Measure (7 days + 1,000 views)
  → Correct the knowledge → Record in EXPERIMENT_LOG.md
  → If proven, write it into factory law; if disproven, write down
    what was wrong and what replaces it
```

Rules of the loop:
- First experiments are **learning instruments, not proof of expertise**.
  A good result on EXP-001 does not make the factory a TikTok
  professional. It makes the factory one experiment wiser.
- Do not wait until the factory "knows everything" before experimenting,
  and do not treat early experiments as evidence of mastery. Learn and
  test in parallel; let the results correct the knowledge.
- When a result contradicts the knowledge, the knowledge is wrong, not
  the result. Update the module, not the numbers.
- Every module ends with a "what this changes in the factory" section:
  concrete rules adopted, or hypotheses queued for testing. A module
  with no consequence for the factory is a reading list, not learning.
- Every module also ends with a "what this module suggests we don't
  know" section: at least two links to other domains, and at least one
  question the module opened that no current domain covers. That section
  feeds the discovery backlog (see the discovery protocol).

## The discovery protocol — how the program rediscovers itself

A permanent practice, not a one-off scan. Discovery Scan 1 was the
first run of it (2026-11-22); the protocol below is what makes the
second, third, and twentieth scans happen on schedule instead of by
accident.

### (a) Horizon scanning — looking beyond the current queue

- **Scheduled scans.** A full horizon scan runs after every two
  completed modules, and at least once a month, whichever comes first.
  The scan is a delegated research task with the same brief as Scan 1:
  find capabilities, techniques, formats, tools, or opportunities
  relevant to the factory that the current domains do not cover.
  Each scan writes a report to
  `research/discovery-scan-N-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, with the same source
  discipline as Module 1, and ends with a "what this scan did NOT
  cover" section — honest gaps are the next scan's starting points.
- **Triggered scans.** A scan is also run immediately when any of
  these happens: TikTok releases a new Community Guidelines version
  or changes Creator Rewards; a new tool, technique, or format
  appears that the current queue cannot place; an experiment result
  contradicts a conception the factory holds; or a module report's
  "what we don't know" section names a gap too large for the queue.
- **Breadth rule.** A scan must search outside the factory's current
  vocabulary. Adjacent fields (psychology, game design, education,
  comedy, community building), emerging production technology,
  distribution and community mechanics, creator-economy developments,
  audience psychology beyond retention, and anything else the map
  does not cover. A scan that only re-reads the current domains has
  failed its brief.
- **No waiting to be told.** The scan does not ask Mohamed or any
  assistant what to learn. It searches, reads, and judges. Its
  verdicts (new module / hypothesis to test / watch item) are the
  factory's own professional judgements, recorded with their evidence.

### (b) Cross-domain linking — how one finding suggests another

- Every module report must name at least two links to other domains:
  what its findings change, constrain, or enable elsewhere. (Example:
  D11 curiosity science constrains D6 hooks and D5 retention; D12
  gamification feeds D7 brand rituals and D15 community.)
- Every discovered domain entry in the log names the domain(s) it
  links to. A domain with no links is a suspicion, not a discovery —
  either it connects to the factory's work or it does not belong in
  the queue.
- When a link is found, the linked domain's entry is updated with a
  note ("see also D__"). The map is a network, not a list.

### (c) Testing unfamiliar ideas — small, cheap, reversible probes

- An idea outside the factory's current conceptions is not debated
  into the ground and not adopted on enthusiasm. It is probed.
- **Desk probe:** a one-day research spike — read the primary
  sources, tag the claims, write the falsifier. Cost: one day.
  Outcome: a module, a hypothesis, or a dropped idea with the reason
  recorded.
- **Experiment probe:** a single-variant experiment under the existing
  protocol (`EXPERIMENTS.md`) — one variable, 7 days + 1,000 views,
  win only on comments/view AND completion together. Cost: one video
  slot. A probe never replaces the control line; the stability rule
  applies.
- Every probe states in advance what would make the factory drop it.
  A probe with no drop condition is not a probe; it is a commitment
  made before the evidence.

### (d) Re-audit triggers — conceptions are re-checked, not defended

A conception is re-audited (its module re-read against current
sources, and its level possibly demoted) when any of these fires:
- **Platform change:** a new Guidelines version, a Creator Rewards
  change, a new TikTok feature, a changed API. The affected module's
  claims are re-checked against the new sources; stale claims are
  struck, not carried.
- **Contradiction:** an experiment result contradicts the module's
  knowledge. The knowledge is wrong, not the result. The module is
  corrected and the correction is logged.
- **New technique:** a scan finds a technique that supersedes or
  invalidates a current capability (e.g. a renderer, a voice, a
  sourcing route). The current capability is evaluated against it on
  evidence, not defended by seniority.
- **Age:** any claim whose source is older than the platform's last
  relevant change is re-checked before it is relied on again. A claim
  that was true in 2026 and is still cited in 2027 without a re-check
  is a stale claim, not knowledge.
- A re-audit that demotes a domain's level is progress, not failure.
  Record the demotion, the trigger, and what replaces the old
  knowledge.

### Discovery backlog

Questions and gaps that are not yet domains live here until a scan or
a module resolves them. From Scan 1 (2026-11-22):
- **Accessibility** — captions for deaf/hard-of-hearing viewers,
  colour-contrast and font-size minima, flashing-content safety,
  TikTok's own accessibility features. No standard has been checked
  against the factory's captions. Deserves a focused pass before the
  first public video.
- **US law beyond TikTok's policies** — FTC endorsement/advertising
  disclosure rules, COPPA-adjacent questions (content must not
  target children), state privacy law, music-licence enforcement
  practice in the US. Scan 1's licence notes are a starting point,
  not legal advice.
- **Voice / TTS frontier** — no systematic check of free TTS voices
  beyond Kokoro (newer open-weight voices, voice-cloning legality,
  multi-voice dialogue). The voice decision is still provisional
  (af_heart); Scan 1 does not resolve it.
- **TikTok's own 2026 product changes after M1** — Scan 1 relied on
  secondary sources for Series/LIVE/Shop thresholds; every threshold
  must be re-verified against TikTok's own Help Center / Creator
  Academy at the moment of use. No threshold in Scan 1 is stable.
- **Competitor / niche-format intelligence** — no systematic viewing
  or measurement of what quiz/riddle accounts actually do on TikTok
  today, because that needs TikTok contact, which the limits forbid.
  The niche-discovery reports remain the evidence base for formats.
- **Posting cadence, timing, and volume strategy** — deliberately not
  covered by Scan 1: M1 already adopted "no volume strategy" as a
  rule, and no credible data was found that would overturn it. It
  remains an open question for M7 (performance analysis) to answer
  from the factory's own data.
- **Non-English / non-US expansion** — out of scope by decision
  (English-only, US audience); no assessment was made of whether the
  formats would transfer.
- **Depth of the practitioner sources** — most practitioner claims in
  Scan 1 (thumbnail lift percentages, Shop earnings, Duolingo churn)
  are stated without published methodology. They are recorded as
  [PRACTITIONER] or [UNPROVEN] for that reason, and none of them is
  used as a rule anywhere.

## The domains

The domains below are the **current map, not the territory**. D1–D10
are the starting point (Mohamed's list plus two factory-added domains).
D11–D17 were discovered by Discovery Scan 1 (2026-11-22) and are
recorded in the Discovered-domains log below. Future scans will add
more. A domain's number is the order it was discovered, not a ranking.

### D1 — TikTok platform mechanics
**What the factory must be able to DO:** explain, from TikTok's own
documents, how a video gets from upload to a viewer's For You feed;
predict which of our videos are eligible for recommendation and which
are not; keep every published video inside the platform's rules.
**Current level:** NONE (Module 1, done 2026-11-22, is the first study).
**Key questions:** What are the stages of the recommendation system?
Which signals does TikTok say it uses, and which does it say it does
*not* use? What makes a video ineligible for the For You feed? What
does TikTok not disclose about ranking?
**Sources:** Tier 1 first — Newsroom "How TikTok recommends videos",
Safety Center recommendation-system page, Community Guidelines.
**Becomes a capability via:** `research/tiktok-mastery-1-2026-11-22.md`
(Module 1); rules adopted into `DESIGN_RULES.md` and the publishing
checklist in `EXPERIMENTS.md`.

### D2 — TikTok SEO
**What the factory must be able to DO:** choose, for each video, the
topic phrase real people search for; place it in the caption, on
screen, and in the spoken script so the video is findable by search;
read Creator Search Insights and find content gaps; measure search
traffic per video.
**Current level:** NONE.
**Key questions:** What does TikTok officially confirm about search
ranking, and what is practitioner inference? Do captions, on-screen
text, and spoken audio all feed search — and with what weight? How do
content gaps work, and how are they found? How much of a video's value
is search value under Creator Rewards?
**Sources:** Tier 1 — Newsroom "Creator Search Insights", Newsroom
Creator Rewards (search value as a formula metric). Tier 3 for the
three-layer (caption / on-screen / audio) practice, flagged as
practitioner until our own experiments test it.
**Becomes a capability via:** a keyword routine added to the video
build (caption + on-screen + script carry the same topic phrase);
a search-topic queue in the experiment log; Module 4 report.

### D3 — Google SEO for video
**What the factory must be able to DO:** judge whether a TikTok-hosted
video (or a page about it) can appear in Google search results; know
what indexing and appearance require; decide whether a web presence is
worth building for the factory at all.
**Current level:** NONE.
**Key questions:** Does Google index TikTok video pages, and for what
queries? What does Google require for a video to appear in video
results (structured data, thumbnails, transcripts)? Is a factory
website a discovery asset or a distraction at this stage?
**Sources:** Tier 1 — Google Search Central (video indexing, VideoObject
structured data). Tier 3 for practitioner claims about TikTok-on-Google
visibility.
**Becomes a capability via:** Module 8 report; a decision (with
reasons) on whether the factory builds any web presence. No website is
built before this module — a public site is also a precondition TikTok
may demand for API app review, which is a separate, documented fact.

### D4 — Audience study, especially the US audience
**What the factory must be able to DO:** describe the target viewer —
what they care about, how they behave on TikTok, what they expect from
a quiz or riddle video, what feels natural and what feels foreign;
write questions, jokes, and references that land with a US audience
instead of reading as translated or generic.
**Current level:** NONE. The factory's English is untested against a
real US audience; Mohamed's judgement alone is not a substitute (he
has said so himself).
**Key questions:** Who watches quiz/riddle content on TikTok, and why?
What do US viewers expect from a 90-second quiz round? Which
references, phrasings, and humour styles read as natural, and which
read as imported? What do comments on comparable videos actually say?
**Sources:** Tier 2 — published audience data (Pew, TikTok's own
published usage data, large-sample creator analytics). Tier 3 — comment
analysis on comparable videos (read, not copied). Our own account's
comments and retention data, once they exist, outrank all of it.
**Becomes a capability via:** an audience profile document (written
after Module 3, updated from our own data); writing rules in the
script templates; Module 3 report.

### D5 — Retention science
**What the factory must be able to DO:** explain, with evidence, why a
viewer stays or leaves at each second of a video; design the pacing of
a round (question → think → reveal) so completion rises; read a
retention curve in TikTok Studio and say what to change next.
**Current level:** NONE. The factory has a 5-second think time (trivia)
and a 10-second think time (riddles) chosen by intuition, not evidence.
The H3 prediction in `EXPERIMENTS.md` (pace beats suspense) is a
guess waiting for data.
**Key questions:** What does the retention curve of a quiz video look
like, and where do viewers leave? Does a longer think time raise
completion (suspense) or lower it (boredom)? What does a rewatch mean,
and what causes one? Which is worth more: a viewer who finishes once,
or a viewer who rewatches half?
**Sources:** Tier 2 — published retention research where it exists.
Tier 3 — practitioner retention analyses with data. Tier 1 — TikTok
Studio's own retention metrics (once the account exists). Our own
retention curves are the deciding evidence.
**Becomes a capability via:** pacing rules in `DESIGN_RULES.md`
(think-time values, reveal timing, rewatch triggers); hypotheses in
the experiment queue; Module 2 report.

### D6 — Hooks, of all kinds
**What the factory must be able to DO:** open a video in a way that
stops the swipe, in several distinct ways — visual, audio, textual,
narrative — and choose the hook that fits the video instead of
repeating one template; test hooks against each other and keep the
winners.
**Current level:** NONE. The current prototypes open with a title card
("TRIVIA CHALLENGE") and a spoken introduction. No hook has been
tested against any other.
**Key questions:** What kinds of hooks exist, and what does each one
promise the viewer? Which hook fits a quiz round and which fits a
riddle round? Does the hook live in the first 1 second, the first 3,
or the whole opening beat? What is a hook that is a lie (clickbait the
video does not deliver) — and why is it banned by our own rules?
**Sources:** Tier 3 — hook taxonomies from practitioners, treated as
inventories to test, not recipes to follow. Tier 2 where a study
measures hook performance. Our own experiments decide which hooks
work for *our* format.
**Becomes a capability via:** a hook library in `factory/` (named hook
patterns, each with its promise and its falsifier); hook wording as
the first variable in the experiment queue; Module 2 report.

### D7 — Brand and identity
**What the factory must be able to DO:** build an account a viewer
remembers, searches for, and follows for its value — a name, a visual
system, a series structure, a voice, and a promise that is the same in
every video and different from every other quiz account.
**Current level:** NONE. The factory has a visual system (safe zone,
icons, accent colour, progress track) but no brand: no account name,
no series identity, no stated promise, no reason for a viewer to
follow rather than just watch.
**Key questions:** What is the account's promise in one sentence? What
makes a viewer follow a quiz account instead of watching one video and
leaving? What does "distinctive" mean when the format (quiz) is
crowded — where does the difference live? How does a series structure
(streaks, rounds, scores) turn viewers into returners?
**Sources:** Tier 2 — published brand research where applicable.
Tier 3 — analyses of distinctive accounts (mechanics, not imitation).
Our own follows/view and return-viewer data, once they exist.
**Becomes a capability via:** a brand document (name, promise, visual
and verbal identity, series structure); identity rules in
`DESIGN_RULES.md`; Module 6 report.

### D8 — Craft: storytelling, design, motion, voice, editing, performance analysis
**What the factory must be able to DO:** write a round that holds
curiosity without artificial confusion; design a frame that reads in
one glance; move elements with purpose; choose and direct a voice that
sounds natural to a US listener; cut a video so no second is dead air;
and read performance data and say what to change.
**Current level:** NONE as professional craft. The factory has proven
*technical* craft (deterministic rendering, word-timed captions, safe
zone) and an unproven voice (af_heart, listening test unanswered).
Technical craft is not creative craft.
**Key questions:** What makes a quiz round a story rather than a list
of questions? What does the current motion vocabulary lack, and which
gaps matter for retention? Does the voice sound natural to a US
listener — and who decides? What does a good edit feel like at 90
seconds?
**Sources:** Tier 1 for the tools' own documentation (Kokoro, OpenFilm).
Tier 3 for craft practice. The listening test and the audience's
retention data are the deciding evidence for voice and pacing.
**Becomes a capability via:** script templates; motion primitives in
`factory/icons.py` and the renderer; voice decision after the
listening test; Module 7 report.

### D9 — Research and fact-checking for content (factory-added)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** verify every question, answer,
fact, and riddle before rendering, from a checkable source; keep a
source record per video; never present a fabricated claim as fact.
**Current level:** NONE. The prototypes were fact-checked against
reliable sources and the sources recorded in `prototype-1/REPORT.md`
— that is manual practice on two videos, not proven professional
expertise. No systematic method exists yet.
**Key questions:** What counts as a reliable source for a trivia
answer? How is a source recorded so a later correction is traceable?
What happens when a published video is found to contain an error?
**Sources:** Tier 1 — the sources themselves (encyclopaedias,
official records). Tier 1 — TikTok's misinformation policy (a wrong
"fact" presented as fact is a policy risk, not just an embarrassment).
**Becomes a capability via:** a fact-check checklist in the build
process; a sources file per video; a correction procedure; Module 9
report.

### D10 — Account operations and standing (factory-added)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** keep the account in good
standing; read TikTok Studio (retention, traffic, search, monetization
status) and record numbers verbatim; respond correctly to a strike,
a removal, or a review; never do anything that risks the account.
**Current level:** NONE. The legitimate connection paths, the Studio
metrics, and the prohibited automation are documented in
`research/tiktok-connection-2026-11-22.md` — that is desk research,
not proven professional expertise. No account exists yet, so no part
of it has been practised.
**Key questions:** What does each TikTok Studio screen actually show,
and which number answers which question? What is the appeal process,
and when is it used? What account behaviours put standing at risk?
**Sources:** Tier 1 — TikTok Help Center, TikTok Studio itself,
Community Guidelines (enforcement). Tier 1 — the Creator Rewards
review/appeal rules.
**Becomes a capability via:** a Studio reading routine in
`EXPERIMENTS.md` §4; an operations checklist; Module 10 report.
Pulled forward immediately if any strike, removal, or review occurs.

### D11 — Curiosity-gap and retrieval-practice science (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** design questions whose gap a
viewer can feel (knows enough to sense it, not enough to close it);
hold the gap the right length; write reveals that close it cleanly;
and know when curiosity helps memory and when it blocks what follows.
**Current level:** NONE.
**Key questions:** Which gap sizes hold attention without frustrating?
How long should a reveal be held? Does a confusing answer waste the
curiosity the question built? What does the 2024 interference finding
(high curiosity for one question *hurting* memory for unrelated facts
shown right after) mean for what the factory places after a reveal —
a CTA, a fact, the next question?
**Sources:** Tier 2 — Gruber et al. 2014 (*Neuron*); Roediger &
Karpicke 2006; Karpicke & Roediger 2008 (*Science*); 2024 *npj Science
of Learning* interference study. Full citations in Scan 1 (C1).
**Links to:** D5 (retention — what holds attention), D6 (hooks —
what opens the gap), D9 (fact-checking — the answer must be right,
or the gap closes on a lie).
**Becomes a capability via:** question-design rules (gap size, reveal
timing, post-reveal placement); Module 3 report.

### D12 — Gamification and habit architecture (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** turn a one-off quiz into a
returning habit — daily rounds, streaks, score-keeping, difficulty
ladders — using loss aversion and variable rewards honestly, without
driving shallow speed-running or engagement gaming.
**Current level:** NONE.
**Key questions:** What makes a viewer come back tomorrow rather than
watch once? Which streak/score mechanics fit a quiz format, and which
corrupt it (streak-protection gaming, XP optimised for quantity over
quality)? Where is the line between a ritual and a compulsion loop
the factory will not build?
**Sources:** Tier 3 — Duolingo gamification case (churn figures from
SEC filings, practitioner analysis); Tier 2 — 2016 *Frontiers in
Psychology* streak study; 2025 meta-analysis on game-based quiz
formats. Full citations in Scan 1 (C2).
**Links to:** D7 (brand — rituals are identity), D15 (community —
leaderboards and shared streaks), D5 (retention — a single video vs
a returning viewer are different problems).
**Becomes a capability via:** a return-loop design (streak/score
visuals rendered deterministically, ritual structure); Module 4 report.

### D13 — Why people follow and share: parasocial attachment, social currency, identity (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** explain why a viewer follows
an account and why they send a video to someone else — two different
decisions from finishing a video — and design for them without adding
a human face the factory does not have.
**Current level:** NONE.
**Key questions:** What can a non-human persona do instead of a face:
a consistent voice, a mascot/character system, recurring rituals,
direct address? What makes a quiz video shareable *as identity* ("I
got 9/10 — can you beat me?") rather than merely watchable? What does
parasocial attachment cost a faceless channel?
**Sources:** Tier 3 — Horton & Wohl 1956 (parasocial, via secondary
summaries); Goffman on self-presentation; practitioner writing on
social currency and sharing psychology. Full citations in Scan 1 (C3).
**Links to:** D7 (brand — attachment is what a brand *does*), D15
(community — sharing is participation), D4 (audience — who shares,
and to whom).
**Becomes a capability via:** a follow/share design (persona system,
share prompts, identity framing); Module 9 report.

### D14 — Sound design and loudness (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** mix every video to a measured
loudness target with ducking and timed effects — dialogue first, SFX
second, music last — and verify it automatically, the way the
safe-zone checker verifies layout.
**Current level:** NONE. Every video so far is voice-plus-silence,
with no loudness target, no ducking, no SFX. Audio fatigue may be
costing retention the factory currently attributes to visuals or
scripts.
**Key questions:** What loudness target and ducking rules fit a phone
speaker in a noisy room? Which sounds (pop on reveal, whoosh on
transition, riser before the final question) help, and which distract?
How is the audio layer verified automatically (FFmpeg `ebur128`)?
**Sources:** Tier 3 — practitioner consensus on −14 LUFS integrated,
true peak under −1 dBTP, ducking 6–12 dB; TikTok publishes no loudness
target. Supply side (C6): Openverse CC0/CC-BY, SFX Kit CC0, Stable
Audio 3 Small SFX — all to be licence-verified per file before use.

**Becomes a capability via:** a measurable audio spec (LUFS target,
ducking rules, SFX timing) enforced by an automated FFmpeg check;
Module 10 report. C6 (sourcing) folds in as its supply chapter once
the C6 hypothesis is tested.

### D15 — Community and participation mechanics (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** turn comments into the next
video's premise — reply-to-comment videos, Q&A, a comment bank —
and run a weekly ritual that gives followers a reason to return,
without letting reply culture become unsustainable.
**Current level:** NONE. No account exists yet, so no part of it has
been practised. The *system* (comment bank schema, reply-selection
rules, ritual template) can be designed before the account exists.
**Key questions:** Which comments make genuinely useful standalone
content, and which do not? How many reply videos per week, and what
cap keeps the loop sustainable? Does publicly rewarding commenting

**Sources:** Tier 3 — practitioner frameworks for the comment-to-content
loop; the growth claim itself is [UNPROVEN] (no controlled data found
in Scan 1). The mechanic is native to TikTok; the claim that rewarded
commenting begets commenting is logic, not evidence.
**Links to:** D12 (gamification — rituals and streaks), D13 (follow/
share — participation is attachment), D10 (account operations — the
comment bank is sketched during M15).
**Becomes a capability via:** a comment bank and reply routine;

### D16 — Monetisation ladder beyond Creator Rewards (discovered, Scan 1)
**What the factory must be able to DO:** know every income rung
(LIVE, Video Gifts, Subscriptions, Series), what each requires, what
each implies as a product (a live quiz format, a premium Series), and
how the rungs interact — especially where they conflict.
**Current level:** NONE. Nothing in it can be acted on before the
account exists and approaches 1,000 followers.
**Key questions:** Which rung is reachable first, and what does it
require the factory to build? What does a premium Series look like
for a quiz factory, and what does it cost the free line? Which rungs
interact, and where do they conflict?
**Sources:** Tier 3 — practitioner guides checked against TikTok's
own pages (thresholds: LIVE 1,000 followers; Subscriptions and Video
Gifts 10,000; Series up to 80 videos, $0.99–$189.99, price
unchangeable). **Every threshold must be re-verified against TikTok's

**Links to:** D1 (platform mechanics — Creator Rewards is the first
rung), D10 (standing — fraud and fake engagement rules bind every

### D17 — Cross-platform distribution: Shorts and Reels (discovered, Scan 1)

vertical video is worth adapting for YouTube Shorts and Instagram
Reels, and if so, adapt it per platform (safe zones, caption placement,

cross-posting a watermarked file.

**Sources:** Tier 2 — Metricool Short Video Report 2026 (6M+ videos,
vendor dataset, not peer-reviewed). Tier 3 — practitioner

Full citations in Scan 1 (C10).
**Links to:** D3 (Google SEO — Shorts compounds into long-form
discovery), D2 (TikTok SEO — search behaviour differs per platform),
D1 (platform mechanics — each platform's recommendation differs).
**Becomes a capability via:** a per-platform adaptation spec and a
costed go/no-go decision; Module 13 report. No cross-posting before
that spec exists.

## Rules adopted from Module 1 (official evidence)

1. **Completion stays a deciding metric.** TikTok itself names
   finishing a longer video as the strongest signal.
2. **The AI-generated label stays mandatory on every video.** TikTok
   *requires* it for realistic AI audio/video.

   minute, and TikTok reports 50% of viewing time is on videos longer
   than one minute.

   force is **released 25 August 2026, effective 24 September 2026**.
   Re-check on any new release.

   paid TikTok Series do not earn Creator Rewards. No free episode is
   ever linked into a Series without an explicit decision weighing the

## What "professional" means here

Not a certificate and not a feeling. A professional factory is one that:
1. Knows what it does not know, and says so (every domain above starts
   at NONE, in writing).
2. Can state, for any decision, whether the evidence is an official
   document, published data, a practitioner claim, or our own measured
   result — and acts accordingly.
3. Learns from its own market, not from imitation: the account's data
   outranks every guru.
4. Turns learning into durable capability: a rule, a template, a
   module, a check — not a memory in a chat.
5. Keeps learning after every win. No successful style is final; no
   module is the last one.
6. **Discovers what it does not know.** It scans beyond its current
   map, links findings across domains, probes unfamiliar ideas
   cheaply, and re-audits its conceptions when the evidence changes.
   A factory that only learns what it was told to learn is a student,
   not a professional.

The loop (OPERATIONS.md) runs this file: each run reads the module
queue, does the next learning step, and records what changed — and
each scheduled scan re-checks whether the queue itself is still the
right queue.
