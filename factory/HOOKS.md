# Hook library — patterns for trivia and riddle videos

**Source module:** Module 2, `research/retention-hooks-2026-11-22.md` (2026-11-22).
**Status:** LEARNING. None of these patterns has been tested on our format. Each pattern
is an inventory item to test, not a recipe to follow. One pattern per video; the winner
is decided by 3-second retention, then completion — never by taste.

**Rules every pattern obeys (Module 2, rules 1–5):**
- The promise is spoken AND on screen by second 3 (design to 2, deadline 3).
- Something moves in frame 0. No static title card as the opening.
- The hook passes the promise test: the video delivers the exact thing it promised.
- The hook survives muted viewing: the promise is text on screen, not voice-only.
- The question stays visible ≥3 seconds; the first change lands before second 3.

**How to test a pattern:** one video, one pattern, same format as the control.
Primary metric: 3-second retention. Secondary: completion. Falsifier per pattern below.
A pattern that loses 3-second retention against the control by any clear margin is
dropped for that format; a pattern that wins must win again on a second video before it
becomes a default.

---

## 1. The Direct Question

**Promise:** "This question has an answer, and you can get it — right now."
**Opening line (spoken + on screen):** "Only 1 in 10 people get this one right. Can you?"
**Then:** the question itself, immediately, on screen for ≥3 seconds.
**Delivery requirement:** the question must be genuinely hard-but-solvable, and the
answer must arrive in the video. A question nobody can answer is not a hook, it is a
wall.
**Fits:** trivia first; riddles second (a riddle's question is the riddle itself).
**Falsifier:** 3-second retention at or below the control's — the question did not
create a reason to stay.

## 2. The Stakes (timer / score)

**Promise:** "You are being timed, and the score will be kept."
**Opening line:** "Five questions. Sixty seconds. How many can you get?" — with a
visible timer starting at frame 0.
**Then:** question 1, with the timer running.
**Delivery requirement:** the timer must be real (it counts down through the round) and
the score must be given at the end. A fake timer is a lie and fails the promise test.
**Fits:** trivia (rounds, scores); riddles weakly (a riddle round has no natural score
unless one is invented — do not invent one just to use this pattern).
**Falsifier:** viewers leave during the first think time — the stakes did not convert
into attention, or the timer added pressure without fun.

## 3. The Teased Answer

**Promise:** "The answer is surprising, and you will see it."
**Opening line:** "The answer is NOT what you think — and it's coming in 60 seconds."
**Then:** the question, with a visible hint that the answer defies expectation.
**Delivery requirement:** the answer must actually defy the obvious guess. If the
obvious guess is right, the hook lied.
**Fits:** trivia with counter-intuitive facts; riddles whose answer reframes the clues.
**Falsifier:** completion falls — viewers stayed for the tease, then the answer
disappointed them, or the tease made the middle feel like padding.

## 4. The Impossible Claim

**Promise:** "Something you believe is wrong, and this video proves it."
**Opening line:** "Nobody can name all five — and I'll prove it."
**Then:** the five items, one by one.
**Delivery requirement:** the claim must be checkable in the video (the five items are
actually named, or the viewer is shown why they cannot be). An unprovable boast is
clickbait.
**Fits:** trivia (lists, "most people fail"); riddles rarely.
**Falsifier:** comments calling the claim false, or a mid-video dip at the point the
claim is tested — the claim did not survive contact with the content.

## 5. The Common Mistake

**Promise:** "You probably get this wrong — here is the fix."
**Opening line:** "Most people answer this wrong. Here's why."
**Then:** the wrong answer first (named, not mocked), then the question.
**Delivery requirement:** the "most people" claim must be true for the question (a
known common error, fact-checked), and the correction must be real. Never invent a
fake majority error.
**Fits:** trivia with known misconceptions; riddles with a classic wrong turn.
**Falsifier:** 3-second retention holds but completion falls — the mistake hooked
curiosity the video then failed to satisfy.

## 6. The Streak Challenge

**Promise:** "Your streak is on the line, and this round counts."
**Opening line:** "Day 3 of the streak. Get all five and it continues."
**Then:** question 1, with the streak shown on screen.
**Delivery requirement:** only usable once a series exists (M4 territory). A "streak"
with no history is fiction. Do not use before the account has a real series.
**Fits:** trivia series; riddle series.
**Falsifier:** follow/return-viewer rate does not move — the streak is a label, not a
reason to return. (Needs M4's metrics; do not judge this pattern on 3-second
retention alone.)

## 7. The Reveal Rewind

**Promise:** "You just saw the ending. Now watch how it happened."
**Opening line:** the reveal moment first (the answer, the reaction), then: "Here's how
we got there."
**Then:** the question/clues, replayed with the answer known.
**Delivery requirement:** the rewind must add something — a clue the viewer missed, a
twist in the reasoning. Replaying the same content with the answer known is dead time.
**Fits:** riddles (the answer reframes the clues); trivia weakly.
**Falsifier:** the mid-video dip — viewers who saw the ending leave instead of
rewinding with us.

## 8. The Personal Dare

**Promise:** "This is addressed to you, specifically, and it dares you."
**Opening line:** "Pause the video. Try it before I reveal the answer."
**Then:** the question, with a visible pause beat (countdown) before the reveal.
**Delivery requirement:** the dare must be answerable in the pause given, and the
reveal must follow the pause — never reveal during the dare. The pause is think time
with a purpose, not dead air: the countdown is visible and moving.
**Fits:** riddles (the dare is the solve); trivia (the dare is the recall).
**Falsifier:** retention falls inside the pause — the dare felt like waiting, not
playing. Compare against the H3 think-time test (§4.2 of the module report).

## Patterns deliberately NOT in this library

- **Greeting / introduction** ("Hey guys, welcome back") — delivers no promise; the
  evidence says it is the most expensive opening there is.
- **Logo / title card first** — names the format, not the promise; banned as an opening
  by Module 2 rule 2.
- **"You won't believe…" without a specific promise** — a lie-shaped hook; fails the
  promise test by construction.
- **Shock / disgust / fear hooks** — outside the factory's red lines and beside the
  point for a knowledge format.

## Maintenance

- A pattern is added only with a promise, an opening line, a delivery requirement, and
  a falsifier — four parts, no exceptions.
- A pattern's record is updated after each test: date, video, 3-second retention,
  completion, verdict. The library is a log, not a menu.
- Re-audit after M3 (curiosity-gap science) and after the first Studio curves: any
  pattern our data contradicts is struck through in writing, with the data cited.
